THE NEXT DOOR · CHAPTER 15

A Few Days Away

Dev left for Manali on Saturday morning.
Anvi was standing outside her house, watering the plants, when she saw him loading his bag
into the car.
"Going already?" she asked.
Dev looked at her. "Good morning to you too."
"Good morning. Now answer the question."
"Yes, we're leaving. Rohan is waiting."
Anvi nodded. "Have fun. And try not to annoy your friends as much as you annoy me."

Dev laughed. "I'll try. No promises."
His mother came outside with a small bag of snacks.
"Dev, take this. And call me when you reach."
"Okay, Mom."
He put the bag in the car and turned towards Anvi.
"Take care of the house while I'm gone," he said.
Anvi raised an eyebrow. "Why? Is the house going somewhere?"
"I was talking about my mother. She'll probably miss having someone to talk to."
"Your mother can manage perfectly well without me."
"Let's see."
He got into the car, waved goodbye, and drove away.
Anvi went back to watering the plants.
The first day passed normally.
Anvi went to college, returned home, spent some time with her brother, and helped her
mother with dinner.
In the evening, she received a picture from Dev.
It was a photograph of the mountains, with clouds covering the tops.
Dev: Look what you're missing.
Anvi: Nothing special. I've seen mountains before.
Dev: You could've just said it looks nice.
Anvi: It looks okay.
Dev: You're impossible.
Anvi: Enjoy your trip, Dev.
He sent a laughing emoji.

She put her phone aside and continued watching television.
On Sunday, Dev didn't message her.
On Sunday, Dev didn't message her.
Anvi didn't think much of it. He was on a trip with his friends, after all.
But on Monday evening, while sitting outside with her mother, she noticed his mother's
phone ringing.
"Yes, beta? Reached the hotel?" she asked.
Anvi glanced up.
"Good. Have something to eat. And don't stay out too late."
After ending the call, Dev's mother looked at Anvi.
"He says the weather is lovely there. He's enjoying himself."
"That's nice," Anvi replied.
Her mother smiled. "You can ask him yourself. He'll be happy to hear from you."
"Why would I ask him? He's on holiday."
Her mother laughed and went inside.
Anvi stayed outside for a little longer.
On Tuesday, Dev finally texted.
Dev: Alive?
Anvi: Unfortunately, yes.
Dev: Good. I was worried the neighbourhood had become peaceful.
Anvi: It has. Very peaceful, actually.
Dev: That's hurtful.
Anvi: You'll survive.
Dev: Coming back tomorrow.
Anvi: Congratulations.

Dev: You don't sound very excited.
Anvi: Should I arrange a welcome party?
Dev: At least some sweets would be nice.
Anvi: I'll think about it.
She smiled at the screen before putting her phone away.
The next evening, Anvi was helping her brother with his homework when a car stopped
outside.
She looked through the window.
Dev was back.
He was carrying his bag towards the house, looking tired.
A few minutes later, the doorbell rang.
Anvi opened the door.
"Welcome back," she said.
Dev looked at her. "That's it?"
"What else?"
"Where are my sweets?"
"Your mother has probably kept some inside."
"You're a terrible neighbour."
"And you're a very demanding one."
He laughed.
"Anyway, how was everything here?"
"Fine. Nothing happened."
"Did you miss the peace and quiet?"
"More than you can imagine."

Dev shook his head.
"Good to be home," he said.
Anvi smiled slightly. "Go inside. You look tired."
"Good night, Anvi."
"Good night."
She closed the door and returned to the living room.
Her brother looked up from his notebook.
"Was that Dev bhaiya?"
"Yes."
"Is he going to play badminton tomorrow?"
Anvi looked at him.
"He just came back. Let him rest."
Her brother nodded and returned to his homework.
Anvi sat beside him, helping him with the next question.
Later that night, her phone lit up.
Dev: Forgot to tell you something.
Anvi: What?
Dev: I brought you something from Manali.
Anvi: Why?
Dev: Because I wanted to.
Anvi: What is it?
Dev: You'll find out tomorrow.
Anvi: I don't like surprises.
Dev: You'll survive.

Anvi smiled at his reply.
Anvi: Good night, Dev.
Dev: Good night, neighbour.
She put her phone on the bedside table and turned off the light
