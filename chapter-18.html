THE NEXT DOOR · CHAPTER 18

Home Is Where You Are

The next few days felt a little different.
Anvi and Dev still talked, still joked, and still argued over the smallest things. But somewhere
between their conversations, the news of Dev moving away had started bothering Anvi.
Neither of them mentioned it again.
It was Sunday afternoon.
Anvi was sitting in the garden when Dev came outside.
"Hey."
She looked up.
"Hey. What's up?"

"Nothing. Just came to check whether you're still alive."
She laughed.
"Unfortunately, yes."
Dev sat on the chair beside her.
"Free today?"
"Why?"
"Let's go out somewhere. Just for a while."
Anvi thought for a moment.
"Okay. Let me ask Mum."
A little later, they were walking through a nearby market.
They stopped at a café, ordered something to eat and spent almost an hour talking.
Dev told her about his college days, his friends and some of the things he wanted to do in
the future.
Anvi shared a few of her own plans too.
At one point, Dev looked at her and asked, "Can I ask you something?"
"Depends on the question."
He smiled.
"Are you going to miss me?"
Anvi stopped stirring her drink.
"That's a very confident question."
"Just asking."
She looked at him.
"Yes, Dev. I will."
His smile softened.

"Really?"
"Don't make me repeat it."
He laughed quietly.
"Okay."
After a moment, Anvi asked, "What about you?"
Dev looked at her.
"What about me?"
"Will you miss me?"
He smiled.
"Anvi, you're probably the most irritating person I've met."
She rolled her eyes.
"Forget it."
"But I'm going to miss you."
She looked at him again.
This time, neither of them laughed.
A few days later, Dev's family confirmed their move.
They were leaving in ten days.
The news spread quickly through the neighbourhood, and both families decided to meet for
dinner before they left.
That evening, Anvi helped her mother prepare food while her younger brother kept asking
Dev when he would return.
"Dev bhaiya, you'll come back, right?"
Dev smiled.
"Of course, champion. I'll visit."
"Promise?"

"Promise."
Anvi watched them from the kitchen doorway.
Her mother noticed her expression.
"Everything okay, beta?"
She nodded.
"Yeah, Mum."
Her mother smiled gently.
"People move, Anvi. But that doesn't mean relationships have to end."
Anvi looked at her mother.
"I know."
The following evening, Dev asked Anvi to meet him outside.
He was standing near the garden with his hands in his pockets.
"What's so important?" she asked.
"Come, sit."
They sat on the small bench near the gate.
For a moment, Dev seemed unsure how to begin.
Then he finally spoke.
"Anvi, I wanted to tell you something before I leave."
She looked at him.
"Okay."
"When I first moved here, I honestly thought you were impossible to deal with."
Anvi laughed.
"Same."
"Remember our first argument about the car?"
"How could I forget? You were blocking our entire gate."

Dev laughed.
"And then you started calling me irritating every five minutes."
"Because you were."
He smiled.
"Some things haven't changed."
They both laughed.
Then Dev became quiet.
"Anvi, somewhere along the way, you became someone I started looking forward to seeing
every day."
Anvi looked at him without saying anything.
"I don't know exactly when it happened.
Maybe during those badminton games, or those random conversations, or when we'd just sit
outside doing nothing."
He paused.
"But I know that I don't want to leave things between us unsaid."
Anvi lowered her eyes for a moment.
"Dev..."
"I like you, Anvi. Not just as my neighbour or my friend. I genuinely like you."
She remained quiet.
Dev continued.
"And I know I'm moving away. I don't expect you to make any promises or decisions because
of that. I just wanted to be honest."
Anvi looked at him.
"You know what's funny?"
"What?"

"When you first came here, I couldn't stand you."
He laughed.
"Thank you. Very romantic."
She smiled.
"But now, you're one of the people I talk to about almost everything. And honestly, I don't
want that to change either."
Dev looked at her.
"Does that mean...?"
Anvi smiled.
"It means I like you too, idiot."
For a few seconds, neither spoke.
Then Dev laughed.
"Finally!"
Anvi laughed too.
"Don't get too happy."
"Too late."
He gently held out his hand.
"Friends?"
Anvi looked at him.
"We're way past that, Dev."
He smiled.
"Then what are we?"
She took his hand.
"Let's figure it out together."
The next ten days passed quickly.

On the morning of their departure, Anvi stood outside with her family while Dev's belongings
were loaded into the truck.
His younger brother was not part of the family, but his mother had already exchanged
numbers with Anvi's mother, promising to stay in touch.
Dev walked towards Anvi.
"So, that's it."
She smiled.
"Looks like it."
"Don't forget me."
"Hard to forget someone who spent the first week fighting over parking."
He laughed.
Then his expression softened.
"I'll call you when we reach."
"You better."
He looked at her for a moment.
"Take care of yourself, Anvi."
"You too, Dev."
He hugged her briefly before getting into the car.
Anvi waved as the car slowly disappeared down the street.
Three months later
It was a Sunday afternoon.
Anvi was sitting in the living room when her younger brother suddenly shouted from the front
door.
"DI! COME OUT!"
"What happened?"

"JUST COME!"
She walked towards the entrance.
And stopped.
Dev was standing outside with a suitcase.
Anvi stared at him.
"What are you doing here?"
Dev smiled.
"Visiting my favourite neighbour."
She laughed.
"You're back?"
"Yeah. Dad's work arrangement changed, and we're moving back here permanently."
Anvi's smile grew.
"Seriously?"
"Seriously."
Before she could say anything else, Dev's mother came out from behind him.
"Surprise, beta!"
Anvi immediately hugged her.
"Welcome back, aunty!"
Her younger brother was already pulling Dev towards the house.
"Come inside! We have so much to talk about!"
Dev looked at Anvi.
"Missed me?"
She smiled.
"A little."

"Only a little?"
"Don't push it."
He laughed.
That evening, both families sat together for dinner.
There was plenty of food, laughter and conversation.
After dinner, Anvi and Dev stepped outside for some fresh air.
They sat on the same bench where they'd had so many conversations.
Dev looked at her.
"So, neighbour. Everything back to normal?"
Anvi smiled.
"Almost."
"What's missing?"
She looked at him.
"You still owe me a proper date."
Dev laughed.
"I was waiting for you to ask."
"Then you've been waiting for too long."
He smiled.
"Tomorrow?"
Anvi nodded.
"Tomorrow."
Dev reached for her hand, and she held it.
For a while, they sat quietly, enjoying the evening.
The same two houses stood beside each other, the same street was filled with familiar
sounds, and the same people were sitting together.

Only one thing had changed.
They were no longer just the neighbours who couldn't stand each other.
They were Anvi and Dev.
And this time, neither of them had to say goodbye.
