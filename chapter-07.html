THE NEXT DOOR · CHAPTER 07

The Weekend Plan

It was Saturday morning.

Anvi was sitting at the dining table, having breakfast with her family.
Her younger brother was busy watching cartoons on his phone while her father read the
newspaper.
"Anvi, what are your plans for today?" her mother asked.
"Nothing much. Why?"
"I need a few things from the market. Can you bring them in the evening?"
Anvi nodded.
"Sure, Mum."
Her younger brother suddenly looked up.
"Di, can we go to the park today?"
"Again?"
"Please!"
"Let's see."
Just then, the doorbell rang.
Her brother jumped out of his chair.
"I'll open it!"
He ran towards the door and returned a few seconds later.
"DEV BHAIYA!"
Anvi looked towards the entrance.
Dev walked inside with a smile.
"Good morning, everyone."
"Good morning, beta," Anvi's mother replied.
"Come, have breakfast," her father offered.
"No, uncle. I've already eaten."
Dev looked at Anvi.

"Morning, neighbour."
She took another bite of her breakfast.
"Morning."
Her brother immediately started talking.
"Dev bhaiya, are you free today?"
Dev looked at him.
"Depends. Why?"
"Let's go to the park!"
Dev smiled.
"Sure. In the evening?"
"YES!"
Anvi shook her head.
"Both of you should find something better to do."
Dev pulled out a chair and sat down.
"Why? Jealous?"
"Of what?"
"Our friendship."
Anvi laughed.
"Please. I'm just worried about your influence on him."
Dev placed a hand on his chest.
"That hurts."
Her father laughed at their conversation.
"Anvi, let them enjoy. You should also go with them."
Anvi looked at her father.

"Dad!"
"What's wrong? It's just a park."
Dev smiled.
"See? Uncle understands me."
"Don't get too happy," Anvi replied.
By six in the evening, the three of them were at the park.
Her brother ran towards the swings while Anvi and Dev walked behind him.
"He's got a lot of energy," Dev said.
"Unfortunately, yes."
Dev laughed.
They found a bench and sat down.
For a while, they watched her brother playing with some other children.
"So," Dev started, "what are you studying?"
Anvi looked at him.
"Are you seriously asking me this after living next door for weeks?"
"I've been busy."
"Doing what?"
"Unpacking, settling in, surviving your attitude..."
She laughed.
"Very funny."
Dev smiled.
"Now tell me."
Anvi told him about her studies and the subjects she enjoyed.
Dev listened, occasionally asking questions.

After a while, she asked, "What about you?"
"I'm working on a few things. Still figuring out some stuff."
"That's vague."
"I'll explain properly some other time."
Anvi nodded.
"Fair enough."
It was the first time they had sat together without arguing every other minute.
A little later, her brother came running towards them.
"Dev bhaiya! Come play football!"
Dev stood up.
"Let's go."
Anvi stayed on the bench.
"Anvi di, you also come!"
"No, I'm fine."
Dev looked back at her.
"Come on. You can't sit here all evening."
"I'm comfortable."
"That's just an excuse."
She gave him a look.
"Go play."
Dev shrugged.
"Your loss."
He followed her brother.
Anvi watched them play for a while.

Dev was surprisingly patient with the children. Whenever her brother missed a shot, he
encouraged him instead of making fun of him.
She smiled slightly and looked away.
Around seven, they started walking back home.
Her brother was telling them about all the goals he'd scored.
"Dev bhaiya said I'm getting better!"
"You are," Dev replied.
Anvi looked at her brother.
"Just don't forget your homework tomorrow."
His smile disappeared.
"Di!"
Dev laughed.
"Let him enjoy his Saturday."
Anvi turned towards Dev.
"Don't spoil him."
"Too late."
They reached their houses.
Dev stopped near his gate.
"Had a good time?"
Anvi nodded.
"Yeah. It was nice."
Dev smiled.
"Good."
Her brother waved at him.

"Good night, Dev bhaiya!"
"Good night, champion."
Anvi turned towards her house.
"Good night, Dev."
"Good night, Anvi."
Later that night, Anvi was sitting on her bed when her best friend called.
"Hello, madam! What's happening?"
"Nothing. Just got back from the park."
"Alone?"
Anvi laughed.
"No, with my brother and the new neighbour."
"Oho! The neighbour again."
"Don't start."
"I'm just asking."
"There's nothing to ask. He's actually not that bad."
There was a brief silence.
Then her friend started laughing.
"WHAT?"
Anvi immediately corrected herself.
"I mean, he's tolerable."
"Sure, Anvi. Whatever you say."
"Good night. I'm cutting the call."
Her friend was still laughing when Anvi ended it.
She placed her phone on the bedside table and switched off the light.
