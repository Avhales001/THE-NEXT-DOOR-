THE NEXT DOOR · CHAPTER 17

The Day That Mattered

The week passed quickly, and Anvi barely had time for anything other than college,
rehearsals, and preparing for the cultural event.
On Friday evening, she was standing in front of her mirror, trying to decide what to wear.
Her phone buzzed.
Dev: Ready for tomorrow?
Anvi: Almost. Still deciding what to wear.
Dev: That's a serious problem.
Anvi: Very funny.
Dev: Don't worry. You'll do well.
Anvi: You haven't even seen my performance.
Dev: I don't need to. I know you'll do well.
Anvi read the message twice before replying.
Anvi: Thank you.
The next morning, the college campus was busier than usual. Students were running
around, teachers were checking arrangements, and the auditorium was almost full.
Anvi stood backstage with her best friend, looking slightly nervous.
"Stop checking your phone," her friend said.
"I'm not checking it."
"You've checked it five times in the last two minutes."
Anvi put her phone away.
Just then, a message appeared.

Dev: Reached college?
Anvi: Yes. Are you coming?
Dev: Obviously. I said I'd come.
Anvi smiled and slipped her phone into her bag.
A few minutes later, the performances began.
Anvi's turn was still some time away. She sat with her friends, watching the other students
perform, but her attention kept drifting towards the entrance.
"He's probably on his way," her friend whispered.
"Who?"
Her friend gave her a look.
"Nobody," Anvi replied.
When her name was finally announced, she took a deep breath and walked onto the stage.
The lights were bright, and the audience seemed much bigger from there. For a moment,
she felt nervous.
Then she noticed someone standing near the back of the auditorium.
Dev.
He gave her a small thumbs-up.
Anvi smiled and turned towards the stage.
Her performance went better than she had expected. When it ended, the audience
applauded, and her friends cheered loudly from their seats.
As soon as she came backstage, her best friend pulled her into a hug.
"You did it!"
Anvi laughed. "I know!"
"Someone was watching very carefully."
"Please stop."
Her friend laughed and walked away to meet the others.

Anvi picked up her phone and saw a message.
Dev: Told you.
Anvi: You actually came.
Dev: I did say I would.
Anvi: Thank you for coming.
Dev: You looked happy up there. That's enough.
Anvi was still reading the message when Dev appeared near the backstage entrance.
"Congratulations," he said.
"Thank you."
"You were good."
"Just good?"
"Fine. Very good."
She laughed.
They walked outside together, away from the noise of the auditorium.
"Are you free now?" Dev asked.
"Yes. Why?"
"Come, let's get something to eat. You haven't had a proper meal all day, have you?"
Anvi looked at him.
"How do you know everything?"
"Your mother told mine. Our mothers share information better than news channels."
She laughed, and they walked towards a nearby café.
They ordered sandwiches and cold coffee.
For a while, they talked about the event, her performance, and how her best friend had
somehow managed to convince half the college to participate.

Then Dev's phone rang.
He looked at the screen and his expression changed slightly.
"I'll just take this."
He stepped outside.
Anvi continued drinking her coffee, but when he returned, he seemed quieter than before.
"Everything okay?" she asked.
"Yeah."
"Are you sure?"
Dev looked at her for a moment before sitting down.
"There's something I need to tell you."
Anvi put her glass down.
"What happened?"
Dev took a breath.
"My father's work situation has changed.
He's been offered a position in another city."
"Oh."
"We're probably going to move."
Anvi didn't say anything immediately.
"When?" she finally asked.
"Next month. Maybe a little later. Nothing is completely final yet."
She looked down at the table.
"That's good, right? If it's a better opportunity for him."
"Yeah. It is."
"Then you should go."

Dev nodded, but neither of them spoke for a few seconds.
"Why didn't you tell me earlier?" she asked.
"I wasn't sure it would happen. And I didn't know how to bring it up."
Anvi gave a small nod.
"Okay."
"That's all?"
"What do you want me to say, Dev?"
"I don't know."
She looked at him.
"You haven't even decided when you're leaving, and you're already making it sound like
goodbye."
Dev looked away.
"I didn't want you to hear it from someone else."
"I appreciate that."
They finished their food, but the conversation wasn't as easy as it had been before.
When they reached home, Dev stopped near his gate.
"Anvi?"
She turned around.
"Hmm?"
"I'll still be here for a few more weeks."
"I know."
"And I'll see you tomorrow?"
She gave him a small smile.
"Of course. Your badminton partner isn't going anywhere."
Dev smiled too.

"Good."
Anvi walked towards her house. Before entering, she looked back once.
Dev was still standing near his gate.
He raised his hand, and she did the same.
That evening, Anvi sat on her bed with the little mountain keychain Dev had brought for her.
She turned it between her fingers while looking at the messages on her phone.
A few minutes later, it buzzed.
Dev: Reached home safely?
Anvi: Dev, I live next door.
Dev: Just asking.
Anvi: Yes, I reached safely.
Dev: Good night, Anvi.
Anvi: Good night.
She placed the phone beside her and looked at the keychain again.
For the first time, the thought of Dev not living next door didn't feel like a small change.
And she wasn't sure what to do with that feeling.
