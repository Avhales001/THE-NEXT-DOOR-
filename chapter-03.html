THE NEXT DOOR · CHAPTER 03

An Unwanted Favour

It was Saturday afternoon.
Anvi was sitting on the living room sofa, scrolling through Instagram while her younger
brother played a video game beside her.
"Di, I'm hungry."
"Then eat something."
"Make something for me."
"Your hands are broken?"
He looked at her.
"Please?"
Anvi sighed and got up.
Just as she entered the kitchen, her mother called her.
"Anvi, can you go to the market and bring some groceries? I've made a list."
"Now, Mum?"
"Yes, beta. I'll start preparing dinner."
Anvi took the list and grabbed her scooter keys.
A few minutes later, she was outside trying to start her scooter.
Nothing happened.
She tried again.
And again.
"Oh, come on!"
She got off and checked the scooter, hoping the problem would somehow fix itself.
"Having a bad day?"

Anvi turned around.
Dev.
Of course.
"Do you ever mind your own business?"
Dev was standing near his gate, holding a water bottle.
"Just asking."
"Then don't."
He looked at the scooter.
"Not starting?"
Anvi gave him a look.
"No, I'm just standing here for decoration."
Dev laughed.
"Okay, okay. Need help?"
"No, I can manage."
"Clearly."
Anvi ignored him and tried starting it one more time.
Nothing.
Dev put his bottle down and walked closer.
"Move aside. Let me check."
"I said I can manage."
"And I said let me check."
She stepped back reluctantly.
Dev examined the scooter for a moment before trying the ignition.
"Looks like the battery is having an issue."

Anvi looked at him.
"Are you a mechanic?"
"No, but I know a little about vehicles."
"Very impressive."
Dev looked up.
"Do you want help or a conversation?"
"Just fix it."
He smiled slightly and tried a few things, but the scooter still wouldn't start.
"Okay, I think you'll need to get it checked."
Anvi sighed.
"Great. Just great."
"Where are you going?"
"Market."
"I can drop you."
She immediately shook her head.
"No, thanks."
"Why? I'm not asking you to marry me. It's just a ride."
Anvi stared at him.
"Do you ever stop talking?"
"Not usually."
She checked the time on her phone. Her mother was waiting for the groceries, and walking
to the market would take too long.
"Fine. But just to the market."
Dev picked up his keys.
"Your wish, neighbour."

A few minutes later, they were on their way.
Anvi sat quietly in the passenger seat, checking the grocery list on her phone.
Dev glanced at her.
"You're always this quiet?"
"When I'm not talking to irritating people, yes."
"Good to know."
They stopped at a traffic signal.
Dev noticed her looking outside.
"So, what do you do?"
Anvi turned towards him.
"What?"
"I mean, are you studying or working?"
"Studying."
"What?"
"Why? Are you taking an interview?"
Dev laughed.
"Just trying to have a normal conversation."
"Try harder."
The signal turned green.
Dev shook his head and started driving.
At the market, Anvi got out of the car.
"Thanks for the ride."
"You're welcome."
She started walking towards the grocery store.

"Anvi!"
She turned around.
"What?"
"Don't forget your list."
He held up the small piece of paper she'd left on the dashboard.
Anvi looked at her empty hand.
For a moment, she said nothing.
Then she walked back and took it.
"Thanks."
"Anytime."
She gave him a small nod and went inside.
Dev waited in the car, scrolling through his phone.
Around fifteen minutes later, Anvi returned carrying two grocery bags.
Dev immediately got out and took one from her.
"I can carry it."
"I know. Give me that one."
She handed it over.
On the way back, they barely spoke.
When they reached their houses, Dev placed the bags near her gate.
"Here you go."
"Thank you, Dev."
He looked at her.
"Was that so difficult?"
"What?"

"Saying thank you."
Anvi rolled her eyes.
"Don't get used to it."
Dev laughed and walked towards his house.
Anvi picked up the bags and went inside.
That evening, Dev was sitting outside his house when Anvi's younger brother came running
towards him.
"Dev bhaiya!"
"Hey, champion. What's up?"
"Will you play badminton?"
"Of course. Get your racket."
The two started playing in the street.
After a while, Anvi came outside to collect some clothes from the drying stand.
She noticed them playing.
Her brother spotted her.
"Di! Come play!"
"No."
"Please!"
"Ask Dev. He's free all day."
Dev stopped playing.
"Actually, she's scared she'll lose."
Anvi slowly turned towards him.
"Excuse me?"
Dev smiled.

"You heard me."
She put the clothes aside.
"Give me the racket."
Her brother immediately started cheering.
Dev handed it to her.
"Let's see what you've got."
Anvi took her position.
"Don't cry when you lose."
Dev laughed.
"Sure."
The game started.
And within the first few minutes, Anvi managed to score three points.
Dev looked at the scoreboard.
"Okay. Not bad."
Anvi smiled proudly.
"Still think I'm scared?"
"One good shot doesn't make you a professional."
She served again.
This time, Dev missed.
Her brother burst into laughter.
"DEV BHAIYA LOST!"
Anvi raised her eyebrows.
"Something you wanted to say?"
Dev picked up the shuttlecock.

"Best of three."
"Scared of losing again?"
"Just play."
They continued until the streetlights came on.
By the end, Dev had won two games.
"See? Experience matters," he said, returning the racket to her.
Anvi handed it to her brother.
"Beginner's luck."
"Whatever helps you sleep."
She picked up the clothes and walked towards her house.
"Good night, Dev."
Dev looked at her, slightly surprised.
"Good night, Anvi."
